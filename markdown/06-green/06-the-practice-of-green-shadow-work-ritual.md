## The Practice of Green: Shadow Work Ritual

We're at 30 minutes now—double Red, triple Purple. You're building the capacity for sustained, focused inner work. And the practice that does this most powerfully at Green is **"Coal to Gold" Shadow Work**: a ritual Geoff built for himself and first published as [*Alchemizing Coal to Gold: a Shadow Work Ritual for Self-Love*](https://creekmasons.com/p/shadow-work-f88).

He built it because of a journal entry like this one:

> "To be worthy," my apparently innate but probably experientially derived reasoning articulates, "is to be perfect." To hold these blemishes of character in the same container as the eloquent, compassionate, introspecting, loving, attentive patterns of behavior of which I am proud—that taints the container beyond repair.
>
> My shadow is the bad apple that poisons the bunch.

Maybe you have a version of that entry. Most Liminal Creeps do. The ritual's premise is that the bad apple was never rotten. The shadow characteristics are the inevitable byproduct of the light ones. Go into memory with a pick axe and you pull out chunks of coal cut through with veins of gold. The work is to transmute the coal and put the pieces back, until your picture of yourself shifts from "mostly coal" to "mostly gold."

It draws on four teachers: Marshall Rosenberg (*Nonviolent Communication*), the channeled language of Paul Selig, Jason Louv's pranayama, and Damien Echols' *High Magick*. It's chaos magick in the honest sense: use what works, and drop what doesn't.

### Pregame: The Three-Column List

Before the first session, make the list. You've already started it if you've done Prompts 1 and 2 of Green's journaling. Three columns:

1. **Shadow.** A behavior, characteristic, or identity you've disowned. "Elitist." "Needy." "Gullible." "Explosive."
2. **Memory.** The earliest memory you can recall of that characteristic showing up. Be specific: where you were, who was there, what you felt.
3. **Reframe.** The light that casts this shadow, named as a quality you'd be glad to embody.

The third column is Rosenberg's move. Instead of calling someone "gullible," you can call them "trusting," and look at the practical difference that makes. A trusting person moves through life believing others have their back, and confirmation bias hands them memories that confirm it. A person who believes they're gullible expects to be taken, and finds the evidence for that instead. The characteristic and the situations are the same. What changes is what you embody and convey, and so how you experience your life.

Finding the light behind some shadows is hard. Stick with it. Use a thesaurus if you have to. "Arrogant" might become *confident*. "Needy" might become *connected*. "Explosive" might become *fierce in defense of what matters*. A reframe that doesn't feel true yet is fine. A reframe that's a lie (calling cruelty "honesty") isn't, and you'll know the difference.

Geoff's list had about twenty rows. You won't get through yours in one 21-day stage, and you don't need to. **One row per session.**

### The Ritual (30 minutes)

Work with one row. Have a glass of water within reach and a timer set for 15 minutes.

**Part 1 (optional): Open.** If you know the Lesser Invoking Ritual of the Pentagram, begin with it. Geoff vibrates the god-names associated with emotion or intellect, to give the reframe a chance to really set in. If ceremonial magick isn't your language, sit, breathe, and state your intention for this session in one plain sentence.

**Part 2: Word through the memory (about 3 minutes).** This is Paul Selig's language. Selig is a medium who says these phrases came from his Guides; you don't have to believe that to find out whether they work. Picture the memory from your second column as clearly as you can. See what you saw. Feel what you felt. *Smell what you smelled.* Then, with the memory vivid, read these two paragraphs aloud, putting your Shadow entry in the brackets:

> I am word through this memory and I am word through any manifestations and any belief systems that were created through this memory that are no longer in alignment with my highest good. I am now choosing to release any patterns of **[Shadow column entry]** that may have been created through this situation and through this time. I am word through this memory and all that it has begotten me. Word I am word through this intention. Word I am word.
>
> I am now making the choice to have this cleared once and for all and I intend now to release any unconscious behavior that would have me reclaim this behavior that I have stated I am now free of. Word. I am word through this intention. Word I am word.

**Part 3: Meditate (15 minutes, at least).** Start the timer. Geoff uses Jason Louv–style pranayama here, to keep charging energy and to reach a state of ecstasis in which the next step can really make an impression. Any charging breath you already know works: Red's belly breathing, or the Skull Shining breath from [Orange's alternatives](../05-orange/14-alternatives-for-orange-practice.md). If the memory is still loud, let it be loud. You're not trying to get rid of it. You're building the charge you'll spend in Part 4.

**Part 4: Charge the water and drink it (about 5 minutes).** When the bell rings, go straight to the water. This is Damien Echols' technique from *High Magick*, a cousin of [Red's Charging Water](../03-red/07-alternatives-for-red-practice.md). Hold the glass. Visualize the five spheres of the Golden Dawn's Middle Pillar (a ceremonial map, separate from the course's chakras):

1. Head: a white orb of light
2. Throat: a grey orb
3. Chest: gold
4. Pelvis: purple
5. Feet: black

On each inhale, see beams of light shoot into you from the top point of the universe, down through head and throat, and up from the earth's core through feet and pelvis. They meet in the middle, at the golden heart. There the light turns to gold, and on each exhale it flows down your arms into the glass until the water glows in your mind's eye.

After three to ten breaths, when the water looks good and bright, say aloud, with your Reframe in the brackets:

> I am word through my intention to embody and convey that I am **[Reframe column entry]**. I know myself as **[Reframe]**. Word I am word.

Then vibrate the name *Yeheshuah*, **YAY-ESH-OO-WA**, letting the sound ring through your body. And drink the water.

**Part 5 (optional): Close.** You can close with the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram. If you're not used to doing it daily, know that it can have some interesting effects when you first start. Otherwise, sit for a moment, then write a line or two about what came through. Don't analyze. Document.

### Why It Works

Each session does three things to one memory. Part 2 brings it up vivid and in the body, instead of as a half-remembered wince. Parts 3 and 4 hold it in a charged, focused state. And Part 4 pairs it with a new name, said aloud, drunk down, so the next time that pattern surfaces you have a second label within reach.

Geoff's report, after running the whole list: memories that had arrived for years with a muttered "*Fuck!*" lost their venom. The ritual shifted them into grayscale and shrank them to the size of a postage stamp. When he transgresses in one of those old sectors now, he's quick to apologize and quick to accept forgiveness when it's offered. That's one person's experience, not a promise. But it's the reason this is Green's practice and not an alternative: it doesn't stop at seeing the shadow. It changes what you call it, and what you call it changes what you go looking for.

A few honest cautions. Start with milder rows; the memories that knock you out of your window of tolerance belong with a therapist or trusted guide first, and the ritual can wait for them. Some sessions will feel like nothing happened. Those count. And pair the practice with Green's habit, [Active Recovery](./11-why-active-recovery-is-essential-for-shadow-work.md), because whatever you stir up has to move through the body somewhere.

This is Carl Jung's insight with a magician's toolkit: you don't eliminate the shadow. You find the gold it's been hiding, and you put it back where it belongs.

That's the gift of Green. That's the practice.
